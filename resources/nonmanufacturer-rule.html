Federal Contracting Guide

What Is the SBA Nonmanufacturer Rule?

Small businesses bidding on federal set-aside contracts are usually expected to manufacture what they sell. But most federal industrial supply demand — fasteners, gaskets, valves, safety equipment — doesn't come from small manufacturers. It comes from distributors. The Nonmanufacturer Rule is the exception that makes that legal.

The basic rule

Under 13 CFR 121.406, a small business can bid on a contract set aside for small manufacturers — even if it doesn't manufacture the product itself — as long as it:

  • Is a recognized small business under the applicable NAICS code for wholesale distribution
  • Supplies the product of a small business manufacturer, processor, or producer
  • Takes ownership or possession of the item with its personnel, equipment, or facilities
  • Will provide the product of a domestic small business manufacturer, unless a waiver applies
In plain terms: if you're a distributor sourcing from a qualifying small manufacturer and reselling to the government, you can compete for contracts that on paper look manufacturer-only.

Why this matters for industrial supply

Agencies like the Defense Logistics Agency buy enormous volumes of consumable industrial hardware — fasteners, bearings, hose, welding consumables — on a recurring basis. Very few of these purchases go directly to the factories that make the parts. They go through distributors who can hold inventory, meet delivery windows, and manage the paperwork of federal procurement. The Nonmanufacturer Rule is what makes that distribution model eligible for small-business set-asides in the first place.

What it doesn't do

The rule doesn't waive quality, sourcing, or domestic-preference requirements. A distributor still has to source from a qualifying manufacturer, and depending on the contract, Buy American or Trade Agreements Act rules may still apply to where the underlying product is made. It also doesn't apply automatically to every NAICS code — eligibility is class-by-class, so it's worth confirming for the specific product category before bidding.

The takeaway

For a company built as a wholesale industrial distributor, the Nonmanufacturer Rule isn't a workaround — it's the legal foundation of the entire business model for federal set-aside eligibility. Understanding it precisely is one of the first things worth getting right before bidding on a DLA solicitation.
