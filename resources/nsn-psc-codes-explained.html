Understanding NSN & PSC Codes

Two sets of codes show up constantly in federal industrial supply work: NSNs and PSCs. They look similar — strings of numbers attached to a solicitation — but they identify completely different things, and mixing them up is one of the more common mistakes new suppliers make.

National Stock Number (NSN)

An NSN identifies one specific, exact item — down to the size, material, and specification. It's a 13-digit number made up of a 4-digit Federal Supply Class (FSC) and a 9-digit National Item Identification Number (NIIN). If a solicitation lists an NSN, the buyer wants that precise part, not a close equivalent.

SegmentLengthWhat it identifies
Federal Supply Class (FSC)4 digitsThe general category of item (e.g., fasteners, valves)
National Item Identification Number (NIIN)9 digitsThe exact, unique item within that category

Product Service Code (PSC)

A PSC is broader — it classifies the type of product, service, or research being procured across an entire contract, not one specific part. Where an NSN says "this exact bolt," a PSC says "this contract is generally about fasteners." PSCs are what agencies use for market research, spend analysis, and small-business set-aside classification at the contract level.

The distinction that matters: NSNs identify the item. PSCs classify the procurement. A single contract can cover many NSNs but will usually carry one or two PSCs.

Why this matters for a distributor

When searching for opportunities, PSCs are the useful filter — they let a supplier scan for the categories of business relevant to their product lines without needing to know every individual NSN in advance. NSNs become relevant once a specific solicitation is in front of you and you need to confirm the buyer wants exactly what you can supply.

The takeaway

Get comfortable filtering opportunities by PSC first, then verifying exact specifications by NSN once a solicitation looks like a fit. Confusing the two — or assuming a PSC match means an NSN match — is a common way new suppliers submit quotes on items they can't actually deliver as specified.
